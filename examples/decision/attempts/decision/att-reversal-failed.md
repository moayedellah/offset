# ILLUSTRATIVE — attempt at reversal cost (failed)

## What I wrote

> This decision is reversible.

## Why it failed

One word, and it was doing four jobs. **Reversible** was being asked to mean
"technically possible", "cheap", "fast", and "without regret" — and those come
apart. A decision can be technically trivial to undo and ruinous to undo.

The word was hiding the claim rather than making it. Naming the dimension
("reversal is *possible* but the *cost* is time-dependent") is the difference
between a slogan and something the next reader can check.

## Why this failure is the most valuable note in the vault

`unlocked_by: att-reversal-failed` — this failed attempt is the only reason the
compiled claim was allowed to open at all. Without it, the claim sits `unseen`,
the wrong word stays buried, and the whole vault looks like it is working.

That is the deal the Five Laws are built on: **failure has to be cheap and
routine, or nobody records it and the enforcement is theatre.**
