# ILLUSTRATIVE — reversal cost is time-dependent

## One-line definition

Reversibility is not a property of a decision. It is a property of a decision
*at a point in time*, and the two are not the same thing.

## The claim

Whatever is being decided, the cost of walking it back is not flat: it is
cheap inside the first month and expensive after, because the cost is
accumulated commitments rather than the decision itself.

## Why `kind: inference`

**The source does not say this.** The two conversations contain the raw
material — what people said, what it would take to undo — but the
time-dependence is my construction from them. `kind: inference` is the only
honest label available, and after a few months it is the only thing that will
tell me which of these sentences came from a person and which came from me.

## Proven at `derive`

The proof is the argument that the cost tracks commitments rather than the
choice — see `pr-reversal`. Not the observation that reversing is hard, which is
recall, and not a rule that could be applied to any decision without thinking.
