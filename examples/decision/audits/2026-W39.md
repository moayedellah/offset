# Attention audit — 2026-W39

## Frequencies, not grades

Read from `attention-log.md` in the same vault. A fact,
not a judgement.

> 268 recorded switches, 197 of them under 90 seconds. Longest unbroken block:
> 74 minutes. Median block: 6 minutes. Four sessions exceeded 90 minutes. Two
> days have no entries at all.

## What this does not say

It does not say the week was bad. **214 short switches is unremarkable for a
day of admin**, and I am not permitted to attach an interpretation to that
number, because a number with a judgement welded to it becomes a grade within a
week and a grade is a lie with a chart on it.

The counts are here because the mechanism is real — cognitive activity about a
previous task persists while you work on the next [cite: leroy2009], and recall
performance falls as switch count rises [cite: liefooghe2008]. The magnitudes
circulating in popular accounts of that literature are **not** trustworthy: in a
properly controlled design the cue-repetition effect accounted for nearly
two-thirds of the measured switch cost [cite: wiradhany2021].

And voluntary switching is a different animal — self-initiated switching has
been shown to *reduce* depletion and increase focus [cite: nuhn2026]. This vault
counts switches because they are free to count, not because a target is
warranted. There is no target here. There is no target anywhere in this
repository.
