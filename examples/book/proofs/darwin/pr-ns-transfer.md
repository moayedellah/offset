# Proof — natural selection at `transfer`

**The problem the book never poses.** Two island species share a character that
the nearest mainland relatives lack, and the island species also share a
character with each other that no mainland species shares. Natural selection
alone, operating on the resources actually available, does not obviously
produce both. I was asked to work out what does.

**What I produced:** a founder-effect account that predicts *which* of the two
shared characters would appear first and *how many* founders it would take, then
a selection account that predicts the second character would follow only if
recovery of variation were restored after the bottleneck. The order of the two
characters in the record discriminates between the two accounts, and it orders
them the selection account needs.

**Why this is transfer and not derive:** the reasoning is mine, not the book's.
The book supplies the ingredients; applying them to a case the author never
considered, in a way that makes a prediction that could be wrong, is a different
act from reproducing his argument. This is the rung that earns a subject
milestone.
