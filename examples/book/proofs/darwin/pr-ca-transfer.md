# Proof — common ancestry at `transfer`

**The problem.** Two organisms share no structural character I can find —
different body plans, different tissues, no obvious vestigial organ in either.
Given only that, work backwards to where their lineages must have met.

**What I produced.** Neither "similarity" nor "habitat" nor "complexity" is
usable: all three are unbounded, so all three are consistent with lineages that
never met. The usable constraint is that shared *character-state patterns* are
nested rather than crossing. Build the set of states each lineage could have
had at a hypothetical ancestor, and the lineages must meet at an intersection
of state-sets. The nesting is what forces a common point; the characters
themselves never had to.

**What could have falsified it.** A lineage whose character states require an
ancestor with states no other lineage permits, in a way that cannot be explained
by reversal. I found no such case, which is a weak result and I am recording it
as weak.
