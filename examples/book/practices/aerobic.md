# Practice — aerobic work

## The prescription border

This is the only practice in the vault, and it is here because it is the only
intervention whose evidence survives contact with a hard-nosed reader: a 2025
umbrella review across 133 reviews, 2,700+ RCTs and 250,000+ participants
[cite: bjsm2025]. As we train our body we train our brain — and the honest
version of that sentence carries a dose and a caveat.

Dose, from a meta-analysis of 42 RCTs in adults 45+ [cite: ye2024]: **13–24
weeks** to see an effect, **20–60 minutes** per session, **3–7 days** per week.

Two caveats I am obliged to state, because the numbers are the easy part. The
effect is **modest** — after funnel-plot adjustment general cognition lands at
d = 0.31 [cite: bjsm2025], and a more conservative analysis of RCTs finds
executive function at g = 0.123 [cite: chang2012]. And the dose evidence is
from adults 45 and over; do not silently generalise it to a 25-year-old.

## Proof, not a tick

`proof: proof-aerobic`. A practice is done when there is an artifact, the same
as a claim. What I write after a session is what I noticed — not whether I
went. `treadmill` with nothing observed is a box ticked, and a box ticked is
exactly the manufactured feeling of progress this project exists to refuse.
